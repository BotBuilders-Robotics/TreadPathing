Why not just fork a mecanum follower

Pedro's follower sums four vectors and hands the result to a mecanum mixer. That works because a mecanum robot can move sideways on command. A tank robot cannot: it has three degrees of freedom in the plane and two actuators, and its lateral velocity is identically zero. The only way it closes a lateral error is by turning while moving, which means translation and heading stop being independent channels and the whole vector-summing architecture loses its meaning.

So this is not a port. It is a time-parameterised trajectory follower, because correction authority is proportional to reference velocity, and a separate pose-hold primitive for the endpoint, because no trajectory follower converges at its own endpoint.

What you get

A route builder

Splines, turns, holds, waits, cusps and markers, in a fluent builder that mirrors the planner exactly. Reversed segments are handled for you.

Two controllers

Ramsete and an LTV unicycle controller, both fed by a trajectory that respects velocity, acceleration, centripetal and wheel-speed limits.

Three localizers

Drive encoders, two odometry pods, or a goBILDA Pinpoint — all fusing heading from the IMU, because on a skid-steer chassis wheels give translation and the IMU gives theta.

A tuning ladder

Nine OpModes in order, each measuring one thing and handing you a number. Measure it; do not compute it from the spec sheet.

An offline planner

One self-contained HTML file. Draw a route, watch it drive, and copy out a paste-ready builder chain. Drop a datalog on it to overlay measured pose on planned.

Java 7, on purpose

No lambdas, no streams, no java.time. OnBotJava's Java 8 support is an opt-in checkbox that is off by default, and a lint enforces it on every build.

Installing it

The library is a package tree you drop next to your team code. It is a sibling of org/firstinspires/ftc/teamcode, not nested inside it. Over USB, adb puts the whole thing there in one shot: 

adb push src/org/treadpathing \
         /sdcard/FIRST/java/src/org/
adb push quickstart/org/firstinspires/ftc/teamcode/tread \
         /sdcard/FIRST/java/src/org/firstinspires/ftc/teamcode/

Then Build Everything. On a Control Hub v1.0 running SDK 11.1 the library plus the quickstart compiles in about 23 seconds from cold. In Android Studio, drop src/org/treadpathing into TeamCode/src/main/java/ and it works unchanged.

Writing an auto

Follower follower = Constants.buildFollower(hardwareMap);
follower.setPose(new Pose(9.0, 60.0, 0.0));

Route route = follower.route()
        .splineTo(new Pose(34.0, 60.0, Math.toRadians(0.0)))
        .splineTo(new Pose(52.0, 84.0, Math.toRadians(70.0)))
        .stopAndHold(1.50)
        .turnToDegrees(20.0)
        .reversed()
        .splineTo(new Pose(20.0, 72.0, Math.toRadians(20.0)))
        .build();

follower.follow(route);

Heading is where the robot's nose points, not its direction of travel. On a tank robot those are the same thing up to a sign, and the sign is what reversed() controls.

Honest about what it is

It has run on one robot: a two-motor tank chassis on a Control Hub v1.0, through rungs 0 to 5 of the tuning ladder, and it drove the route it was given. That covers the drivetrain layer, the drive-encoder localizer, the IMU heading fuser, trajectory generation and the follower.

It does not yet cover the odometry-pod or Pinpoint localizers, the LTV controller, or any four-motor chassis — and accuracy on hardware is unquantified, because the rungs that measure cross-track and terminal error have not been run. The known limitations are written down rather than glossed over.
